Light, going up.

93 photographs from four days in the mountains, 4–7 October 2026.

Medhansh NegiOne phone cameraf/1.75, every frame
Statement

Every photograph here comes from one phone camera whose aperture never moved from f/1.75. So shutter speed, ISO and exposure compensation did all the work.

Most of what I pointed it at is mountain light: low sun, cloud, hard shadow, and a few snow peaks. Under each frame is what the camera recorded, and a few words on how the exposure and contrast turned out.

Skills, shown not told

What the controls did

Frame 22 · 1/630 s · −1 EV
Frame 23 · 1/279 s · −1 EV

Same water, two shutter speeds

Both frames are at −1 EV. The slower 1/279 s gives the fall more time to blur into threads; 1/630 s keeps it choppier.

Frame 30 · EV 0
Frame 54 · −⅓ EV

When the sky wins

At EV 0 the sky in frame 30 goes to white. A small −⅓ EV in frame 54 keeps the cloud and lets the ridge fall to silhouette.

What low light costs

Frame 28, shot at 06:00, is the slowest and highest-ISO frame of the set: 1/17 s at ISO 500. At that speed the whole frame blurs from movement, and grain shows in the shadows.

Self-evaluation

What worked, what didn't

What worked

The backlit frames. , and make the sun the subject and let the ridge fall to silhouette. is the cleanest frame: sky, snow ridge, one bird. The −1 EV frames in chapters II and III kept their skies blue.

What I'd fix

Too many frames have something in the way: a dashboard (, ), a wing mirror (, ), wires across the sky (, , ). The blue-hour shadows in , and are heavy, and and lose the sky completely. Almost everything is landscape or sky; people only appear at the edges (, ).

Next shoot

  1. Shoot each scene at −1, 0 and +1 EV, then compare.
  2. Try a slower shutter on purpose for moving water.
  3. Shoot from outside the car, not through the windscreen.
  4. Make one person the subject.
  5. Check the corners for wires and mirrors before pressing.
